How to check bit-perfect playback on a Linux streamer

Guide 11 · 4 min read

Short answer: playback is bit-perfect when every sample reaches the DAC exactly as it is in the file. On a Linux streamer you can check most of it in two minutes, because the system writes down the format the sound card receives. A matching format is necessary, not sufficient: a digital volume control changes every sample and can leave the format as it was.

Checked on 2026-10-17 and 18 with MPD 0.24.5 and 0.24.4, against MPD's manual and source code; the measurements are dated where they appear.

What can change the samples

  • Resampling: 44.1 kHz turned into 48 or 96 kHz.
  • Format conversion: 24 bits cut to 16, or 16 bits widened to 24. Widening keeps each value and adds zeros below it: the DAC receives a longer word carrying the same music.
  • Digital volume below 100 %: every sample is recalculated.
  • Mixing: a sound server shared by several programs (PulseAudio, PipeWire, ALSA's dmix) brings everything to one common format.
  • Processing: an equaliser, room correction, ReplayGain.
  • DSD turned into PCM: if DSD cannot reach the DAC as DSD, a player may convert it to PCM rather than refuse it.

Check 1: what the sound card receives

While a track plays, on the streamer:

cat /proc/asound/cards
cat /proc/asound/card1/pcm0p/sub0/hw_params

The first command lists the sound cards and their numbers: put your DAC's number in the second (card1 here). It answers with lines like these, or closed when nothing plays on that output:

format: S32_LE
channels: 2
rate: 44100 (44100/1)
  • The rate must be the file's. Any other rate means something resampled.
  • The format is the word the card receives: S16_LE is 16 bits, S24_3LE and S24_LE are 24, S32_LE is 32. A 16-bit file arriving as S32_LE has been widened: find out by what (check 2).
  • DSD sent as DSD shows a DSD format (DSD_U32_BE, for instance). Sent as DoP, it shows PCM: DoP wraps DSD in 24-bit PCM words.

Check 2: the player's settings

Many Raspberry Pi streamers play through MPD, and MPD's own manual keeps a check list for bit-perfect playback. In its configuration, the audio_output block that feeds your DAC:

  • device "hw:1,0", straight to the card. A plughw: or default device lets ALSA convert on the way.
  • No mixer_type "software". none leaves the samples alone, hardware hands the volume to the DAC. I measured what the software volume does on MPD 0.24.5 (2026-10-17): even at 100 %, it widens 16-bit audio to 24 bits in a 32-bit word. Every sample of a 20-second test file came out at exactly 256 times its value, the same music in a longer word; a 24-bit file came out byte for byte identical. Towards a DAC that accepts 16 bits only, MPD then brings that audio back to 16 bits with dither: at 100 % too, half the samples of a 3-second test came out changed, by up to 3 steps (2026-10-18). Below 100 %, MPD recalculates every sample: see Software or hardware volume.
  • No ReplayGain, no forced format (format, audio_output_format).
  • For DSD, MPD sends DSD natively when the sound card accepts it, uses DoP only if you set dop "yes", and otherwise converts DSD to PCM. Check 1 shows which happened; the three ways are in DSD on a Linux streamer.

Check 3: a file that only survives untouched

A matching format shows nothing was converted; it cannot show that the values were left alone. Some audiophiles use a DTS-encoded CD track for that: played as ordinary PCM over a digital link to an amplifier or processor that decodes DTS, it is recognised as DTS only if every bit arrives unchanged. I have not run this test myself. If you try it, never play that file through a DAC that does not decode DTS, and start with the volume low: undecoded, it is loud noise.

What Audiogravity shows, and what it does not

Its player shows the file's format, not what the DAC receives. Its signal path (Pipeline tab, Pro) shows the format on each link, and its FULL RES badge compares formats, not samples: it is no proof of bit-perfect playback. Its guided setup sends MPD straight to the card with ALSA's conversions off, as check 2 asks, but with a software volume: at 100 %, your music reaches the DAC as described above.

The details are in the manual: Outputs & engines → The signal path.
