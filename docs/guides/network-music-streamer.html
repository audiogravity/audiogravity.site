Your streamer on the network: how much bandwidth music takes, Wi-Fi or cable, and VLANs

Guide 4 · 5 min read

Short answer: music takes little bandwidth: about 1.4 Mbit/s at CD quality and 9.2 Mbit/s at 24 bits and 192 kHz, before lossless compression shrinks it. Only HQPlayer feeding an NAA at DSD512 or 768 kHz needs around 50 Mbit/s. A steady Wi-Fi link carries the rest; a cable removes the question. What breaks more setups is discovery: AirPlay, UPnP and the NAA find each other with messages that stay on the local network, so a guest Wi-Fi or another VLAN hides devices unless the router passes those messages on.

Checked on 2026-10-22: measured on a Raspberry Pi 4 under Raspberry Pi OS, wired and on Wi-Fi, on a Debian 13 box on Wi-Fi, and on music files; the protocols from their standards, the routers from their makers' documentation, the NAA from HQPlayer's author.

How much bandwidth music takes

Stream, stereo Rate, uncompressed
16 bits, 44.1 kHz (CD) 1.41 Mbit/s
24 bits, 96 kHz 4.61 Mbit/s
24 bits, 192 kHz 9.22 Mbit/s
DSD64 5.64 Mbit/s
DSD128 11.29 Mbit/s
DSD256 22.58 Mbit/s
HQPlayer to an NAA, DSD512 45.16 Mbit/s
HQPlayer to an NAA, PCM 768 kHz, 32 bits 49.15 Mbit/s
  • Lossless compression takes less. FLAC and ALAC carry the same music in fewer bits: in a sample of 147 CD-quality ALAC files, the median needed 861 kbit/s, 61 % of 1,411. Files that are not compressed, WAV and DSF, measured their full rate.
  • HQPlayer sends an NAA its finished output, at the full rate of what it converts to, whatever the file's own rate. DoP or native DSD makes no difference: DoP is handled at the NAA, in HQPlayer's author's words.

Wi-Fi or cable

The Raspberry Pi 4 and 5 have Gigabit Ethernet and dual-band 802.11ac Wi-Fi. To see your Wi-Fi link, on the Pi:

/sbin/iw dev wlan0 link
/sbin/iw dev wlan0 get power_save

On the Pi 4 above, shortened:

signal: -64 dBm
rx bitrate: 150.0 MBit/s
tx bitrate: 24.0 MBit/s
Power save: on

  • The bitrates are what the link is set at, not what it delivers: test what it delivers with iperf3, below.

  • Power saving is on by default on a Raspberry Pi, as above. Raspberry Pi's documentation offers to turn it off in raspi-config, an option "that we don't recommend setting unless instructed to by a Raspberry Pi engineer": it "might improve connection stability but increase power consumption".

  • How steady the link is: with your router's address,

    ping -c 100 -i 0.2 192.168.1.1

    The last two lines give the losses and the slowest answer. The Pi 4 above, pinged from a computer on Wi-Fi, answered in 1.4 ms on average and 2.5 ms at worst through its cable, 5.3 and 7.5 ms on Wi-Fi, with no loss either way.

  • Whether it carries 50 Mbit/s, for an NAA: install iperf3 on the Pi and on another computer (sudo apt install iperf3), run iperf3 -s on the computer, then on the Pi, with the computer's address:

    iperf3 -c 192.168.1.30 -u -b 50M -t 10 -R

    -R has the other computer send, as HQPlayer does. The receiver line gives the rate reached, the jitter and the datagrams lost: losses at 50M mean the link is short for DSD512.

  • For DSD512 and 768 kHz, a cable. HQPlayer's author writes that "Ethernet provides practically unlimited reliable bandwidth with galvanic isolation (NAA works over WiFi as well)"; in one of his tests, DSD512 did not get through Wi-Fi to an NAA where DSD256 did (January 2025).

Finding each other across the network

  • AirPlay announces itself with mDNS: messages to 224.0.0.251, port 5353, an address routers do not pass on.
  • UPnP media servers and renderers announce themselves with SSDP: messages to 239.255.255.250, port 1900, which a router passes on only when set to. On my test network, a media server one router away answered when asked by its address, not to a discovery search (2026-10-21).
  • HQPlayer and its NAA have a discovery of their own, which is not mDNS. Keep them on the same network, and the computer running HQPlayer without a second network connection, such as a VPN: its discovery could go out the wrong way, in its author's words.

So the Apple device, the control app, the server and the streamer must be on the same network: a guest Wi-Fi or another VLAN hides them from each other. To cross VLANs anyway, the router must pass the messages on, and its firewall must let the devices talk:

  • For mDNS (AirPlay), a reflector: Avahi's enable-reflector=yes on a Linux router, the mDNS Proxy of a UniFi gateway, the mDNS Repeater plugin of OPNsense, Enable reflection in pfSense's Avahi package.
  • For SSDP (UPnP), a separate relay: the UDP Broadcast Relay of pfSense and OPNsense, set to 239.255.255.250 and port 1900. pfSense's documentation: "Firewall rules are not automatically created".
  • IGMP snooping, on managed switches, filters multicast: mDNS's addresses always get through, SSDP's may not. Ubiquiti's advice, for audio and video on its switches: "enable IGMP Snooping and define a specific switch as the querier". HQPlayer's author: with such equipment, "the network infrastructure must be up before NAA and HQPlayer are started".

With Audiogravity

Its Network test, in the Performance tab (Pro), runs the same ping, or an iperf3 test at the rate you set, and gives a verdict. In Library → Sources, a UPnP server on another network can be added by its address, which discovery cannot cross.

The details are in the manual: Administration → Network test, and Troubleshooting → A UPnP renderer or media server isn't discovered.
