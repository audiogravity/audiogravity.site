Qobuz, Tidal and HIGHRESAUDIO on a Raspberry Pi

Guide 6 · 5 min read

Short answer: a Raspberry Pi can play all three, but few setups offer all three at once. They differ in what they cost, in the quality they reach, and in where you choose the music: in the service's own app, with Qobuz Connect or TIDAL Connect, or in the streamer's app or a UPnP control app. Each service needs your own subscription, whichever way you choose.

Checked on 2026-10-21 against each product's official pages and source code, read but not run. Prices are those shown that day, and they change: the sources are listed at the end.

upmpdcli, on Raspberry Pi OS or Debian

upmpdcli makes MPD a UPnP renderer and, with one module per service, adds a UPnP media server for Qobuz, TIDAL and HIGHRESAUDIO. You browse it from a UPnP control app, such as BubbleUPnP (Android) or mconnect Player (iPhone, iPad, Android), and the Pi plays.

  • Install upmpdcli and the module of each service, upmpdcli-qobuz, upmpdcli-tidal and upmpdcli-hra, from upmpdcli's site: none is in Debian's archive. The TIDAL module also needs the Python package tidalapi, version 0.8.11, which upmpdcli's manual has you install with pip.

  • Turn each service on in /etc/upmpdcli.conf:

    qobuzuser = me
qobuzformatid = 27
tidaluser = me
tidalaudioquality = HI_RES_LOSSLESS
hrauser = you@example.com
hrapass = your-password

    qobuzuser and tidaluser only switch the service on, whatever their value. 27 asks Qobuz for its highest quality, HI_RES_LOSSLESS asks TIDAL for its. The HIGHRESAUDIO password sits in this file in clear: upmpdcli's manual advises making it readable by its owner only (sudo chmod 600 /etc/upmpdcli.conf). Then restart upmpdcli: sudo systemctl restart upmpdcli.

  • Sign in once to Qobuz and TIDAL. Each module comes with a script that prints a web address: open it, sign in, and the module keeps the authorisation. upmpdcli's manual names the scripts and their options.

  • TIDAL's highest quality reaches MPD only. TIDAL sends it as MPEG-DASH, which MPD plays; other renderers are served CD quality, says upmpdcli's manual.

Volumio 4.231

The three services, Qobuz Connect and TIDAL Connect come with Volumio Premium: €7.49 a month or €69.99 a year, for up to 6 devices, with a MyVolumio account and a 14-day trial. Connect each service under Settings → Sources, with Connect. For TIDAL's highest quality, Volumio's help says to set its quality to Hi-Res. Qobuz lists Volumio among its Qobuz Connect brands.

moOde 10.3.6

  • Qobuz Connect. In Configure → Renderers, under Qobuz Connect, Install builds Pibuz on the Pi itself: about 10 minutes on a Pi 5 with 4 GB, 20 to 40 on a Pi 4, by moOde's own timings. The Qobuz app then plays on moOde; its quality starts at Hi-Res 24/192K.
  • Qobuz as a UPnP media server, through upmpdcli: in Configure → Renderers, Edit on the UPnP renderer, then Qobuz Music Service, with a userid, a password and a quality. You browse it from a UPnP control app. moOde ships upmpdcli 1.9.17, whose manual says that Qobuz now signs in once through a web page, with a script run on the box.
  • No TIDAL and no HIGHRESAUDIO. moOde removed TIDAL from its settings in 2021.

piCorePlayer 11.1 with Lyrion Music Server 9.1

Qobuz and TIDAL come as free plugins from Lyrion's official list: install them under Settings → Manage Plugins, then set each one up in its page under Settings → Advanced.

  • Qobuz 3.7.3: your username and password. Its Preferred format starts at FLAC 16 bits / 44.1 kHz: choose FLAC Hi-Res 24 bits / >96 kHz for its highest.
  • TIDAL 1.8.2: Add Account takes you to TIDAL to sign in. Its Quality starts at AAC 320Kbps and stops at CD (FLAC): no hi-res in this version.
  • No HIGHRESAUDIO plugin in the official list.

HQPlayer and Roon

  • HQPlayer streams Qobuz and HIGHRESAUDIO itself: Signalyst lists both as streaming partners, and you sign in from HQPlayer Client. HQPlayer Embedded runs on a Pi (see HQPlayer Embedded on a Raspberry Pi 5): HQPlayer 6 Embedded costs €319, and plays 30 minutes at a time without a licence.
  • Roon streams Qobuz and TIDAL, as well as KKBOX and nugs, not HIGHRESAUDIO. Its server does not run on a Pi: the Pi plays as a Roon Bridge, and the server runs on a computer (see Roon on a Raspberry Pi). Roon costs $14.99 a month or $149.88 a year.

Check what you get

  • Your subscription sets the ceiling. Qobuz's highest quality, FLAC 24-bit up to 192 kHz, needs its Studio plan: €14.99 a month in France. TIDAL's goes up to 24-bit / 192 kHz.
  • The DAC shows what reaches it. While a track plays, cat /proc/asound/card0/pcm0p/sub0/hw_params gives the rate it receives, rate: 192000 for a 24/192 album played untouched (see How to check bit-perfect playback).

With Audiogravity

The three services come with the Pro edition. They sit beside your own files and your UPnP media servers, in one library and one search. Connect each one from Library → Sources: Qobuz and Tidal through their own sign-in page, HIGHRESAUDIO with your email and password. Audiogravity asks Qobuz and Tidal for their highest quality, and plays on the box's DAC, on a UPnP renderer, or through HQPlayer. There is no Qobuz Connect and no TIDAL Connect: you choose the music in Audiogravity.

The details are in the manual: Library & streaming → Streaming services.
