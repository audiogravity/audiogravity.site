A streamer into an amplifier with DIN inputs: the cable, the level, the volume

Guide 15 · 5 min read

Short answer: many European amplifiers of the 1970s and 1980s, and Naim's up to its latest integrated amplifiers, take their sources on round 5-pin DIN sockets rather than RCA. A DAC connects to them with a cable that has RCA plugs at one end and a DIN plug wired for that amplifier at the other. Which pin carries what is not the same everywhere, so read the amplifier's manual first. These inputs often expect far less than a DAC sends: the volume knob then does its work in the first part of its travel, which is expected, not a fault.

Checked on 2026-10-31 against Naim's manuals for the NAIT 2, its integrated amplifiers of 2019 and the NAIT 50, a DAC board's datasheet and the Linux driver of its chip, with speaker-test from alsa-utils 1.2.14 on Debian 13.

Which pin carries what

A 5-pin DIN socket has its five contacts on a half circle. In the scheme most often given for an amplifier's socket, the amplifier receives the left and right channels on pins 3 and 5, sends a signal out, to a tape recorder, on pins 1 and 4, and has its ground on pin 2. Makers departed from it:

  • some took a source's signal on pins 1 and 4;
  • some fed power on pins 3 and 5, to an external phono stage or to a tuner;
  • Philips and Uher tape recorders used every pin as an input while recording.

So a cable that plays on one amplifier may give a single channel, or nothing, on another. And a socket that carries power must not see its pins joined by a cable wired for something else: on Naim's SUPERNAIT 3 and NAIT XS 3, the aux 2 socket "also carries a power supply output intended to power a Naim StageLine or SuperLine phono preamplifier".

What to do:

  1. Read the amplifier's manual. Naim's draw the sockets with what each contact carries: ch1 for the left channel, ch2 for the right, -ve for the ground, nc for nothing.
  2. Get a cable made for that make, RCA at the DAC's end and DIN at the amplifier's. TNT-Audio advises such a cable over an RCA-to-DIN adapter.
  3. Or check the cable you have, the amplifier off and the cable out: with a multimeter set to continuity, each RCA plug's centre pin must beep with the contact the manual gives for its channel, and each plug's outer ring with the ground.

How much a DAC sends, and what the input expects

A line input's sensitivity is the level that drives the amplifier to full power with its volume turned all the way up. A DAC's datasheet gives its own output at full scale. Two figures from the makers:

Level
Naim NAIT 2, line inputs (Tuner, Aux, Tape) 75 mV
Naim NAIT 50, line inputs, level at clipping 88 mV
HiFiBerry DAC+ Pro, a DAC board for the Raspberry Pi, maximum output 2.1 V

The DAC board sends 28 times the NAIT 2's 75 mV, about 29 dB more. The amplifier reaches full power with its knob far from the top, and the part of its travel you use is short. That is expected, not a fault.

Never connect a DAC to a phono input, DIN or RCA. A phono stage is made for a cartridge: 2.5 mV on the NAIT 2, 840 times less than the board above.

Where to set the volume

  • On the amplifier, with the streamer at 100 %. Your music reaches the DAC untouched, and the amplifier's knob sets the level (see How to check bit-perfect playback).
  • On the DAC's own analogue control, if it has one. Boards built on Texas Instruments' PCM512x chips have a control the Linux driver names Analogue Playback Volume, with two steps: 0 and −6 dB. It lowers the output without changing a sample. The HiFiBerry DAC+ is one: its Linux overlay declares a PCM5122.
  • In the player. A software volume below 100 % recalculates every sample (see Software or hardware volume on a Linux streamer).

Check both channels

Do this once the cable is in, before any music. Turn the amplifier's volume right down first: the test is at a fixed level, and these inputs are sensitive. Stop the player that holds the DAC, MPD for instance (see “Device or resource busy”), then, with your DAC's card number from aplay -l in place of 0:

speaker-test -D plughw:0,0 -c 2 -t wav -l 1

A voice says "Front left", then "Front right", and the terminal shows which it is playing:

 0 - Front Left
 1 - Front Right

Raise the volume slowly. Each voice must come from its own speaker. The two swapped: the cable's channels are crossed. One of them silent: the cable does not reach the right contacts.

With Audiogravity

Its guided setup gives MPD a software volume: leave the player's slider at 100 and set the level with the amplifier's knob. The details are in the manual: Listening.
