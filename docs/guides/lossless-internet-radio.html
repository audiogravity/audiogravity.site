Lossless internet radio: which stations stream FLAC, and how to check what a station sends

Guide 7 · 7 min read

Short answer: most internet radio is MP3 or AAC, which drop part of the music on the way, whatever a station calls itself. Some stations stream FLAC, which drops nothing: of the 88 found here, three in four sent CD quality, 16 bits at 44.1 or 48 kHz, and five went above 48 kHz. Station directories are a poor guide: the largest lists five stations as FLAC, while 82 more of those found sit under "OGG", next to lossy streams. Ask the stream itself: ffprobe names its format in a second.

Checked on 2026-10-26: 333 streams read with ffprobe 7.1 on Debian 13, chosen in the Radio Browser directory (every station listed as FLAC or carrying FLAC, lossless or hi-res in its name or tags, the 150 most-voted stations listed as Ogg, the 150 most-voted listed at 256 kbit/s or more), and eight of them played in MPD 0.24.5 without a sound card. The formats and the directory's workings come from their specifications and source code.

Lossy, lossless, and what names say

  • MP3, AAC, Opus and Vorbis are lossy. Their bitrate, 128, 192 or 320 kbit/s, says how much of the music is kept, not a resolution. A name does not change it: France Inter's "hifi" stream is AAC at 192 kbit/s, BBC Radio 3's "HD Sound" is AAC at 320 kbit/s, and "Classic Vinyl HD" is MP3.
  • FLAC is lossless. Its bits and its rate are the stream's real resolution.
  • Ogg is a wrapper, not a format: it carries FLAC, Vorbis or Opus, and a server sends all three as audio/ogg. Of the 125 most-voted stations listed as Ogg that answered, 56 sent FLAC, 35 Vorbis and 34 Opus.
  • HLS, an address ending in .m3u8, sends the stream in short files. Its specification allows AAC, MP3 and AC-3 on their own, and Apple's adds FLAC and Apple Lossless in MP4 files: SomaFM's Groove Salad sends FLAC that way.

Check what a station sends

ffprobe comes with ffmpeg: sudo apt install ffmpeg. Then, with the station's address:

ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name,sample_rate,bits_per_raw_sample,bit_rate:format_tags=icy-br -of compact "http://stream.radioparadise.com/mellow-flac"

stream|codec_name=flac|sample_rate=44100|bit_rate=N/A|bits_per_raw_sample=16
format|tag:icy-br=1441
  • codec_name is the format: flac is lossless; mp3, aac, opus and vorbis are not.
  • bits_per_raw_sample and sample_rate are the resolution of a FLAC stream. A lossy stream has no bit depth, N/A: its bit_rate, in bits per second, stands in its place.
  • icy-br is the bitrate the station announces about itself: an Icecast server passes on the figure the station's software gives it, and nothing checks it. Naim Jazz announces 128 for the same format as above, 16-bit FLAC.

ffprobe reads a second or two of the stream, follows redirections and reads HLS addresses as they are.

What station directories say

Radio Browser, a free directory to which anyone can add a station, listed nearly 70,000 stations on the day: two in three as MP3, five as FLAC. Its code explains why. A station's codec is what the server's Content-Type names, so every audio/ogg stream is listed as Ogg; its bitrate is the station's own icy-br. Read against the streams themselves:

  • "OGG" hides most FLAC stations. 82 of the 88 FLAC streams found were listed as Ogg, one as MP4 (SomaFM's HLS), five as FLAC.
  • Bitrates are what stations announce. The FLAC streams were listed at anything from 0 to 6,144 kbit/s.
  • Names are not formats. Of the 97 stations that answered and that call themselves FLAC or lossless in their name or tags, or are listed as FLAC, 17 sent MP3, AAC or Vorbis: often a lossy version of a FLAC station, under the same name.
  • "High bitrate" means lossy. Of the 141 most-voted stations listed at 256 kbit/s or more that answered, 113 sent MP3, 23 AAC and one Vorbis; two sent FLAC, and two could not be identified.

So search a directory for "flac" in names and tags, then check each candidate with ffprobe.

Playing them with MPD

mpc add http://stream.radioparadise.com/mellow-flac
mpc play
mpc status '%audioformat%, %kbitrate% kbit/s'

  • An HLS address goes in as it is. MPD 0.24 played SomaFM's and France Inter's .m3u8 from their https:// addresses, 45 seconds each, across several of their files. Written hls+https://, a form MPD's code accepts and some guides give, it failed on SomaFM and played France Inter with decoding errors, and ffmpeg's own log advises the plain address.

  • What MPD shows is "the format emitted by the decoder plugin", in its documentation's words, not what the station sent:

    Station Sends %audioformat%
    Radio Club 80 Trance FLAC, 24 bits, 192 kHz 192000:24:2
    Sveriges Radio P2 FLAC, 24 bits, 48 kHz 48000:24:2
    Radio Paradise, Mellow Mix FLAC, 16 bits, 44.1 kHz 44100:16:2
    Classic Vinyl HD MP3, 320 kbit/s announced 48000:24:2
    France Inter, hifi AAC, 192 kbit/s 48000:16:2
    France Inter, HLS AAC 48000:f:2

    For a lossy stream, the bits are the decoder's working resolution: an app that shows "24 bit, 48 kHz" for an MP3 says nothing of its quality.

  • To reach the DAC untouched, a FLAC stream needs what a file needs: no resampling and no software volume. See How to check bit-perfect playback.

Through HQPlayer

HQPlayer's list of formats has FLAC and MP3, not AAC: "AAC is not supported as it requires additional patent licenses", its author wrote in April 2025. An AAC station reaches it only decoded beforehand, by a player such as Roon, which "always sends just raw decoded PCM or DSD to HQPlayer".

With Audiogravity

Its Radio tab searches the Radio Browser directory, and a station missing from it can be added by its address. A station plays like the music on the box, to the same outputs. For HQPlayer, the box relays each station, and converts to FLAC what HQPlayer does not read: the conversion keeps the sound as it arrived, it does not bring back what a lossy stream left out.

The details are in the manual: Library & streaming → Internet radio.
